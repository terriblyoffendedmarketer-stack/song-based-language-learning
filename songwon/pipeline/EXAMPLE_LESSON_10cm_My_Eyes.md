# Example Lesson: 10cm — My Eyes
## Draft V5 — Korean-immersive (MIA), warm-up activation, tighter Item 3

Song: 10cm - My Eyes
Level: Beginner
Grammar focus: -게 되다 (to end up ~ing)
Lesson position: Lesson 16 (Unit 4)

**Teaching items (2 words + 2 phrases):**
1. 목소리 (voice) — word
2. 목소리가 들리다 (a voice can be heard) — phrase, builds on word 1
3. 보이다 (to be seen / to appear) — word
4. 좋아하게 되다 (to end up liking) — phrase, grammar pattern

**Quiz screens (scored): 15 total**
**Pass threshold: 12/15 (80%)**
**Overall Korean ratio: ~75-80%** (Unit 4 level — earlier units have more English scaffolding)

---

## Immersion Design Notes

### The MIA principle
80-90% target language. The learner should be swimming in Korean, with English only as a life vest — not a boat.

### What's in Korean
- All screen labels and navigation cues
- Quiz prompts (the format teaches itself — no English instruction needed after the first lesson)
- Context sentences (Korean primary, English small below)
- Word/phrase cards (Korean displayed large, English definition small)
- Song lyrics, sing-along, all UI chrome

### What stays in English
- Intro prose (Screens 2-3) — grammar explanations need English for beginners
- Word definitions (the "voice · noun" line — but kept minimal)
- Wrong-answer explanations — must be instantly understood
- English translations below Korean sentences (small, secondary)

### Graduation across units
- **Units 1-2**: English hints on quiz screens, English in wrong-answer explanations, more English scaffolding
- **Units 3-4**: Quiz prompts fully Korean, shorter English explanations, English translations smaller
- **Unit 5**: Near-full immersion — English only for new grammar intro and word definitions

This example is Lesson 16 (Unit 4) — moderate immersion. A Unit 1 lesson would have slightly more English.

## SCREEN 2: Lesson Introduction (music auto-plays)

**Title:** 10cm — My Eyes

Have you ever had someone stuck in your head so hard you start questioning your own sanity? That's what this lesson is about — the moment you realize someone has completely taken over your thoughts, and you can't tell if it's love or if you're just losing it.

Korean has a really useful pair of verbs for this: **들리다** and **보이다**. They're the passive forms of "to hear" and "to see" — but instead of "I hear" or "I see," they mean "it is heard" or "it is seen." The difference matters. When 10cm sings **너의 목소리만 들려**, he's not saying "I hear your voice." He's saying your voice reaches him whether he wants it to or not. It's happening to him. That passive feeling — things happening to you, not by you — runs through the whole song.

*(~30% Korean — this is the grammar explanation, English is necessary here. Korean examples are bolded inline as anchors.)*

---

## SCREEN 3: Lesson Introduction continued (music continues)

**Title:** 이번에 배울 것 *(what you'll learn)*

You'll also learn the grammar pattern **-게 되다**, which means "to end up" doing something or "to come to" a state. **좋아하게 되다** means "to end up liking someone" — not because you chose to, but because it just happened. The singer uses this exact pattern when he asks himself: **내가 너를 좋아하게 되버린걸까?** ("Have I ended up liking you?")

이번 레슨에서 배울 단어:
- **목소리** — voice
- **목소리가 들리다** — a voice can be heard
- **보이다** — to be seen
- **좋아하게 되다** — to end up liking someone

레슨이 끝나면, 이 노래의 첫 번째 구절을 이해할 수 있어요.

*(~40% Korean — the word list and closing line are Korean. Grammar explanation stays English.)*

## ── ITEM 2: 목소리가 들리다 (a voice can be heard) ──

## SCREEN 10: Phrase Card — 목소리가 들리다

*(TTS auto-speaks "목소리가 들리다" on entry)*

**Label:** 새 표현

# 목소리가 들리다
**a voice can be heard** · 표현

들리다 = 듣다의 피동사. "I hear"가 아니라 "is heard" — 소리가 저절로 들려오는 느낌이에요.

이 표현을 통째로 외우세요 — 한국 사람들은 들리다를 혼자 안 쓰고, 목소리가 들리다처럼 써요.

**노래에서:**
너의 [**목소리**]만 넘나 크게 [**들려**]
*(Only your voice sounds so loud)*

*(~80% Korean. Grammar note mixes Korean and English for clarity at Unit 4 level.)*

## ── ITEM 4: 좋아하게 되다 (to end up liking) ──

## SCREEN 22: Pattern Spotlight — -게 되다

**Label:** 패턴을 찾아봐요

# -게 되다
**to end up ~ing / to come to be**

서서히, 자연스럽게, 내가 선택하지 않았는데 그렇게 된 거예요.

**노래에서 (2절):**
내가 너를 좋아하[**게 되**]버린걸까
*Have I ended up liking you?*

**더 많은 예문:**
한국어를 잘하[**게 됐어요**].
*I came to speak Korean well.* (자연스럽게, 시간이 지나면서)

서울에 살[**게 됐어요**].
*I ended up living in Seoul.* (계획이 아니라 그냥 그렇게 됐어요)

*(~70% Korean. Pattern explanation is in Korean, example sentences are Korean with small English translations.)*

### Graduation Model

| Unit | Korean % | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Unit 1-2 | ~60-65% | More English hints on quizzes, English wrong-answer explanations, English UI labels mixed in |
| Unit 3-4 | ~75-80% | Korean quiz prompts, Korean wrong-answer explanations, Korean labels, fewer English hints |

### The Interleaving Pattern

```
WARM-UP (1 screen, not scored) — 복습 or 시작 퀴즈

INTRO (2 screens, music) → LYRICS KR (music) → LYRICS EN (music stops)

ITEM 1 — 목소리 (word):
  teach → quiz → CONTEXT SENTENCE → quiz in context

ITEM 2 — 목소리가 들리다 (phrase, builds on item 1):
  teach → quiz → CONTEXT SENTENCE →
  quiz fill-blank → mix review (item 1) → quiz song line comprehension

ITEM 3 — 보이다 (word):
  teach → quiz → CONTEXT SENTENCE →
  quiz distinguish (들리다 vs 보이다) →
  PAIR CONTEXT SENTENCE (items 2+3 together) →
  quiz song line recall (cumulative)

ITEM 4 — 좋아하게 되다 (phrase + grammar pattern):
  PATTERN SPOTLIGHT → teach phrase → quiz →
  CONTEXT SENTENCE →
  quiz grammar application → quiz comprehension (full Korean options!) →
  CONTEXT SENTENCE (new example)

FINAL MIX (all 4 items):
  4 mixed review quizzes + 1 sentence rebuild

SING ALONG (music, highlighted lyrics) → RECAP (점수 + 통과/재시도)
```

### Wrong Answer Handling

Every quiz screen follows the same pattern on wrong answer:
1. Wrong choice → red background
2. Correct choice → green background (simultaneously)
3. One-line explanation appears at bottom (Korean-first at Unit 3+, bilingual at Unit 1-2)
4. "계속" button to proceed

No retries. No lives. Wrong answers count against the lesson score but never block progress within the lesson. Gating happens at the unit level (need 4/5 lessons passed at 80%+ to unlock next unit).

### Key Principles

1. **Korean immersion (MIA)** — 80%+ target language, English only as a life vest
2. **Immediate activation** — warm-up quiz before any reading
3. **Teach then IMMEDIATELY test** — never more than 1 screen between learning and doing
4. **Context sentence after every test** — Korean primary, English small below, auto-TTS
5. **Phrases as primary vehicle** — teach as chunks, not individual words. "이 표현을 통째로 외우세요"
6. **Escalating difficulty per item** — recognition → fill-blank → discrimination → production
7. **Mix reviews after every 2 items** — forces recall of earlier items
8. **Highlighted words everywhere** — accent color in sentences, song lines, sing-along
9. **Wrong answers explained, never punished** — red/green + brief explanation + 계속
10. **Progression gating at the unit level** — 80% per lesson, 4/5 per unit
11. **Graduation model** — Korean ratio increases across units
